Panelist
Janflor Mc.Louie S. Ullero is an Associate Professor of Philosophy at the School of Arts and Sciences, NU Fairview. He earned his Bachelor of Arts and Master of Arts degrees in Philosophy from the University of Santo Tomas, where he is currently pursuing his Doctor of Philosophy degree. His doctoral research reexamines John Rawls’s theory of justice in light of the ethical, political, and environmental challenges of the Anthropocene. His teaching and research interests span political philosophy, social justice, environmental ethics, human rights, peace education, humanism, and AI ethics. He is particularly interested in how philosophical inquiry can address questions of justice, human dignity, moral responsibility, and the place of human beings in a rapidly changing social and technological world. His interest in humanism extends to questions concerning secular ethics, moral life outside traditional religious frameworks, and the role of reason and critical inquiry in public life. He is also a Research Fellow at the Critical Research Association Melbourne and is affiliated with various national and international philosophical and research organizations. His scholarly work includes “Rawls’ Theory of Justice and the Social Challenges of the Pandemic in the Philippines,” published by Brill. In February 2026, he received support from the British Academy to participate in an Early Career Researcher Workshop on Human Rights Education and Activism in Southeast Asia at Chiang Mai University, Thailand. He was also among the signatories and contributors to the 2025 Bangkok Declaration on the Social, Legal and Ethical Issues of Artificial Intelligence. Outside academia, he enjoys traveling and experiencing different cultures. For him, exploring new places is also an opportunity to encounter different ways of living, thinking, and understanding the world beyond the classroom.
